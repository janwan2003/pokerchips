What blinds are for

The small blind and big blind are forced bets that start the pot before any cards are seen. Without them, nobody has a reason to play a hand. In a cash game the blinds usually stay the same all night. In a tournament they go up at fixed intervals, so stacks shrink relative to the blinds and the game eventually ends.

For a longer game, start at 1/2 blinds. For a short one, start at 10/20.

How long should each blind level be?

  • 15 minutes: a fast game, about 2 hours with 6 to 8 players.
  • 20 minutes: the usual home-game choice, about 2.5 to 3 hours.
  • 30 minutes: a slow, deep game for a long evening.

Count hands instead of minutes if your group plays slowly: raising the blinds every 10 to 12 hands works well.

EasyPoker supports both: set fixed blinds for a cash game, or have the host raise the blinds between levels. Turn on buy-ins when you create the table if your group allows re-buys.

Common blind mistakes in home games

  • Raising the blinds too fast. If stacks are under 10 big blinds within an hour, the game turns into an all-in lottery.
  • Forgetting to raise them at all. A tournament with flat blinds can last until 4 a.m. Set a timer.
  • Blinds that don't match the chips. If the small blind is 5, you need 5-value chips. Use the chip calculator to match chip values to your blinds, or use virtual chips and skip the problem.

Frequently asked questions

What are good blinds for a home poker game?

With a 1,000-chip starting stack, start at 5/10 and raise every 15 to 20 minutes: 10/20, 20/40, 50/100, 100/200 and so on.

How often should blinds go up in a home tournament?

Every 15 to 20 minutes for most groups, or every 10 to 12 hands if play is slow.

What is the big blind usually in relation to the small blind?

The big blind is normally twice the small blind, for example 5/10 or 50/100.

Can I change the blinds in EasyPoker during a game?

Yes. The host can change the blinds at any time from the Host tab.
