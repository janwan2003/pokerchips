How to play poker without chips

You have a deck of cards and a group of friends, but no chip set. Here are seven ways to keep score, from the fastest to the most improvised.

1. Use your phones as chips

The quickest fix, because everyone already has a phone. With EasyPoker, one person creates a table in the browser and shows a QR code. Everyone else scans it, types a name and gets a starting stack. Bets go into a pot that every phone can see, and the winner taps to collect it.

  • Exact stacks, no counting, no change
  • Free, no app download, no sign-up
  • Survives dead batteries and closed tabs; the host can fix mistakes
  • Needs a phone with a browser and some signal for each player

2. Pen and paper ledger

Make a column for each player on a sheet of paper and give everyone the same starting number. One person acts as banker and writes down every bet and every pot. It works, but it is slow: each hand needs several lines of arithmetic, and one mistake throws off every number below it.

Tip: only update the sheet at the end of each hand. Players announce bets out loud during the hand, and the banker writes the final pot result.

3. Coins

Raid the coin jar. Use the coin's face value or agree your own values (for example, small coins are 1, large coins are 5). Coins stack and clack like chips, which keeps the feel of the game. The downside: you need a lot of them, and everyone has to remember what each coin is worth.

4. A second deck of cards

Deal a second deck as currency: number cards are worth 1, face cards 5, aces 10, or whatever values you agree. It is surprisingly playable for small groups. Keep the second deck visibly different (another back colour) so it never gets mixed into the deck in play.

5. Small objects: matches, sweets, beans, pasta

Anything small and countable works: matchsticks, dried beans, pasta shapes, wrapped sweets. Use two or three different objects for different values. It is fun for a casual game and terrible for a serious one, because the objects get lost, eaten or knocked over.

6. Board game money

Paper money from a board game has printed values, which helps. It is fiddly to handle, though: notes blow away, stick together and are hard to count at a glance.

7. Paper chips

Cut squares of paper or card and write a value on each, or use coloured sticky notes for different values. It takes ten minutes of preparation and gives you something closer to real chips than coins or beans.

Frequently asked questions

Can you play poker without chips?

Yes. Chips only track who has what. You can use phones, a written ledger, coins, a second deck of cards or any small objects instead.

What can I use instead of poker chips?

The most practical replacement is a phone chip app, since everyone has a phone. Coins, a second deck of cards, paper chips and a pen-and-paper ledger also work.

How do you play poker with friends without chips?

Give everyone the same starting amount, agree on the blinds, and keep score with one of the methods above. With EasyPoker, the stacks and pot are tracked on each phone automatically.

Is pen and paper good enough for poker?

For a few hands with two to four players, yes. For a full evening it gets slow, and one arithmetic mistake affects every number after it.
