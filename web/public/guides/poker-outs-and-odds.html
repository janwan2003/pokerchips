Poker outs and odds: the rule of 4 and 2

Outs are the unseen cards that would make your hand. Multiply your outs by 4 on the flop (two cards to come) or by 2 on the turn (one card to come) for a quick percentage chance of hitting. A flush draw has 9 outs: about 36% on the flop and 18% on the turn.

How to count outs

After the flop you have seen 5 cards (your 2 and the 3 on the board), so 47 are unseen. After the turn, 46 are unseen. An out is any of those cards that improves you to a hand you expect to win.

  • Flush draw (four of one suit): 13 cards of the suit minus the 4 you can see = 9 outs.
  • Open-ended straight draw (e.g. 8-7-6-5): four cards at each end = 8 outs.
  • Gutshot (inside straight draw, e.g. 9-8-6-5): 4 outs.
  • Pocket pair hoping for a set: 2 outs.
  • Flush draw plus open-ended straight draw: 9 + 8 minus the 2 cards counted twice = 15 outs.

Do not count an out twice, and discount outs that also help your opponent (a card that completes your straight but pairs the board may give someone a full house).

When the rule of 4 is wrong

  • Many outs. Above about 8 outs, ×4 overstates the chance: 15 outs is 54%, not 60%. Use (outs × 4) − (outs − 8) as a closer estimate.
  • Only one card is guaranteed. The ×4 figure assumes you see both the turn and the river. If someone can bet again on the turn, you usually only get one card for the price, so use ×2.
  • Dirty outs. If some outs also improve your opponent, do not count them.

Turning the percentage into a decision

Odds alone do not say whether to call. Compare them with the pot odds: the share of the final pot that your call makes up. If your chance of hitting is higher than that share, calling is profitable in the long run.

At a home game, the arithmetic is easier when the pot is an exact number in front of everyone. With EasyPoker the pot and every bet show live on each phone, so you only have to count your outs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the rule of 4 and 2 in poker?

Multiply your outs by 4 on the flop, or by 2 on the turn, to estimate your percentage chance of hitting by the river.

How many outs does a flush draw have?

Nine. There are 13 cards of each suit and you can already see four of them.

What are the odds of hitting a flush draw?

About 35% from the flop to the river and about 19.6% from the turn to the river.

What are the odds of an open-ended straight draw?

About 31.5% from the flop to the river with 8 outs, and 17.4% from the turn.

What are the odds of flopping a set with a pocket pair?

About 11.8%, or 1 time in 8.5. If you miss on the flop, two outs give about 8.4% to hit by the river.
