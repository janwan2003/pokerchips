Omaha poker rules

Omaha is played like Texas Hold'em, with four hole cards instead of two. At showdown you must use exactly two of your hole cards and exactly three of the five board cards. It is usually played pot-limit, so the biggest raise allowed is the size of the pot.

Omaha in five rules

  1. Each player gets four hole cards face down.
  2. Blinds, the flop, turn and river, and the four betting rounds work exactly as in Texas Hold'em.
  3. Your hand is made of exactly two hole cards plus exactly three board cards. Not one, not three, not four.
  4. Hands rank the same as in Hold'em (see hand rankings).
  5. In Pot-Limit Omaha (PLO), the maximum bet or raise is the size of the pot.

Pot-limit betting, worked out

A pot-sized raise is: call first, then raise by the size of the pot after your call.

  1. The pot is 100. Your opponent bets 50, so the pot is 150.
  2. You first call 50: the pot would be 200.
  3. You may raise by up to 200 more, so your total bet is up to 250.

When nobody has bet yet, the maximum bet is simply the size of the pot.

How Omaha feels different from Hold'em

  • Hands are stronger. With six two-card combinations in every hand, big hands are common. Two pair rarely wins a multi-way pot.
  • Draws are bigger. Wrap straight draws can have 13 or more outs, so the favourite on the flop is often only a slight one.
  • Pots grow fast because pot-limit raises compound on every street.

Omaha at a home game

Frequently asked questions

How many cards do you get in Omaha?

Four hole cards each, plus five community cards shared by all players.

Do you have to use two cards in Omaha?

Yes. Your final hand must use exactly two of your four hole cards and exactly three of the five board cards.

Can you have a flush with one suited card in Omaha?

No. You need two hole cards of the suit, plus three of that suit on the board.

How do you calculate a pot-size raise?

Add the amount you need to call to the pot, then raise by that total. Facing a 50 bet into 100, the maximum total bet is 250.
