Standard poker chip colours and values

There is no official rule, but most home sets and many casinos in the US follow this pattern. If your set has different colours, keep the order: lighter colours for smaller values.

ColourCommon valueUsed for
White1Small blinds in low-stakes games
Red5Blinds and small bets
Green25Standard bets and raises
Black100Bigger bets, later levels
Purple500Tournaments with deep stacks
Yellow1,000Late tournament levels

How many chips per player?

A comfortable starting stack is 50 to 100 big blinds. With 5/10 blinds that is 500 to 1,000 chips in value. Shorter stacks make the game faster and more all-in; deeper stacks give more room to play.

In physical chips, each player needs about 20 to 50 chips, enough small ones to post blinds and make change. That is why chip sets come in these sizes:

Chip setComfortable for
300 chips4 to 6 players
500 chips6 to 8 players
750 to 1,000 chips8 to 10 players, or tournaments with re-buys

Tips for choosing chip values

  • Make the smallest chip equal the small blind. Anything smaller is never used; anything bigger forces change on every hand.
  • Keep each colour 4 to 5 times the one below (5, 25, 100, 500). Fewer colours with big jumps cause change problems; tiny jumps waste colours.
  • Give more small chips than you think. Running out of small chips is the most common home-game slowdown.
  • Colour up as blinds rise. In a tournament, swap the smallest chips for bigger ones once the small blind outgrows them.

Frequently asked questions

How many poker chips do I need for 6 players?

About 300 chips is enough for six players at a cash game, which gives each player roughly 50 chips. For a tournament with re-buys, 500 is more comfortable.

What should the starting stack be in a home game?

Between 50 and 100 big blinds. With 5/10 blinds, a 1,000-chip starting stack is a common choice.

What are the standard poker chip colour values?

A common convention is white 1, red 5, green 25, black 100, purple 500 and yellow 1,000. Home sets vary, so agree on values before the first hand.

How do I count a stack of poker chips quickly?

Count each colour in stacks of 20, multiply by the chip value, and add the colours together. The chip counter on this page does the multiplication for you.
