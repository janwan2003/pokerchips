Pot odds explained

Pot odds compare the size of a call with the pot you could win. Divide the amount you must call by the total pot after your call: that is the share of the time you need to win for the call to break even. Calling 50 to win a pot that will be 200 needs 25%.

The formula

Equity needed = amount to call ÷ (pot before your call + amount to call)

"Pot before your call" includes your opponent's bet. The result is the minimum chance of winning that makes the call break even over many hands.

Worked example

  1. The pot is 100. Your opponent bets 50, so the pot is now 150.
  2. You must call 50. After your call the pot will be 200.
  3. Equity needed: 50 ÷ 200 = 25%.
  4. Expressed as a ratio, the pot is offering you 150 to 50, or 3 to 1.

Putting pot odds and outs together

Say you hold a flush draw on the turn. You have 9 outs and one card to come: 9 ÷ 46 = 19.6% (see outs and odds).

  • Opponent bets a third of the pot: you need 20%. Almost exactly break-even.
  • Opponent bets half the pot: you need 25%. Calling loses chips in the long run, unless you expect to win more later.
  • Opponent bets a quarter of the pot: you need 16.7%. Calling is profitable.

On the flop, if your opponent is all-in so you are sure to see both cards, the flush draw is 35% and you can call a bet up to about the size of the pot.

Implied odds

Pot odds only count the chips already in the middle. Implied odds add what you expect to win on later streets if you hit. With deep stacks and an opponent who will pay you off, a draw can be worth calling even when the pot odds alone say no. The reverse, reverse implied odds, is when hitting your hand can still cost you, such as a small flush against a bigger one.

Pot odds at a home game

The hardest part of pot odds at a live table is knowing the pot. With EasyPoker, the pot is shown as an exact number on every player's phone, so the formula above is one division.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate pot odds?

Divide the amount you have to call by the total pot after your call. Calling 50 into a pot of 150 gives 50 ÷ 200 = 25%.

What are good pot odds to call?

Any time your chance of winning is higher than the equity the pot odds require. Facing a half-pot bet, you need 25%.

What is the difference between pot odds and implied odds?

Pot odds use only the chips already in the pot. Implied odds also include chips you expect to win on later streets.

Should I call with a flush draw?

On the turn, a flush draw wins about 19.6% of the time, so it is a break-even call against a bet of a third of the pot and a losing call against larger bets, before implied odds.
