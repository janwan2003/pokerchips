How to host a poker night

To host a poker night you need a table, a couple of decks of cards, a way to track chips, an agreed buy-in and blind structure, and a short list of house rules. Sort those out before people arrive and the game runs itself.

Last updated 2026-11-12

1. Choose the format

Cash gameTournament
Best forCasual nights, people arriving and leavingA fixed start time and a clear winner
BlindsFixed all nightRise every 15 to 20 minutes
Running out of chipsBuy in againYou are out, unless re-buys are allowed
LengthAs long as you likeAbout 2.5 to 3.5 hours for 8 players

For a first poker night, a tournament is easier to explain and has a natural end.

2. Invite the right number of people

Six to nine players is the sweet spot for one table of Texas Hold'em. Fewer than five gets repetitive; more than ten means long waits between hands, so split into two tables. Invite one or two more than you need, because someone always cancels.

3. Shopping list

  • Two decks of cards with different backs, so one can be shuffled while the other is dealt.
  • Chips: about 50 per player for a set, or none at all if everyone uses a phone with EasyPoker.
  • A dealer button: a coin or bottle cap works.
  • A table big enough to reach the middle, ideally with a cloth so cards slide.
  • A timer for tournament blind levels (any phone clock).
  • Food that is not greasy: finger food leaves marks on the cards. Bowls and napkins away from the felt.

4. Set the stacks and blinds

Start everyone with 50 to 100 big blinds. A common home setup is 1,000 chips with 5/10 blinds, raising to 10/20, 20/40, 50/100 and so on. The blind structure guide has a full schedule, and the chip calculator works out how many chips of each colour to hand out if you use a physical set.

5. Agree the house rules before the first hand

Five minutes of rules saves an hour of arguments. Decide on re-buys, the minimum raise, whether you can show one card, and what happens on a misdeal. Our home poker house rules page is a list you can read out or send to the group chat.

6. Run the night

  • Start on time; latecomers can join a cash game or buy in during the first level of a tournament.
  • Rotate the deal every hand, with the button moving left.
  • Take a short break every hour or two.
  • Keep phones for chips only if you are using them as chips; otherwise keep them off the table.

7. Settle up

At the end, write down each player's final stack before anyone leaves. If you play for stakes, convert chips back at the same rate as the buy-in and settle between yourselves. For tournaments, see how to split a tournament prize pool. EasyPoker never handles money, but every stack and every action of the night stays on record for the host.

Frequently asked questions

How many people do you need for a poker night?

Six to nine players is ideal for one table. Five works; above ten, use two tables.

What do I need to host a poker night?

Two decks of cards, a dealer button, a table, and chips or a phone chip app. Add a timer for tournaments.

How long does a home poker tournament last?

With eight players, 1,000-chip stacks and 20-minute blind levels, about two and a half to three and a half hours.

What is a good buy-in for a home game?

One that nobody minds losing. Agree it in the invite so there are no surprises.

Do I need a poker chip set?

No. Everyone can keep their stack on their own phone with a free browser table like EasyPoker.
