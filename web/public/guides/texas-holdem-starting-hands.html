Texas Hold'em starting hands

There are 169 different starting hands in Hold'em. At a typical home game, play the strongest 15 to 25 percent of them: big pairs, big aces and suited broadway cards, more hands from late position and fewer from early position.

A simple tier list

This is a common, deliberately simple way to group hands for a full-ring home game, not a solver chart. "s" means suited, "o" offsuit.

Why position changes everything

Players who act later see what everyone else did first. That makes marginal hands easier to play, so the later your position, the more hands you can open. A hand like K♣ J♦ is a fold under the gun at a full table and a raise on the button.

Home game adjustments

  • Loose tables (many players call pre-flop): play fewer weak aces and more suited and connected cards and small pairs, which make strong hands that win big multi-way pots.
  • Tight tables (most players fold): steal the blinds more often from late position.
  • Short-handed (five or fewer players): every hand goes up a tier, because fewer opponents hold something better.

Starting hands people overvalue

  • Any ace. A♦ 7♣ loses to every better ace and is hard to play after the flop.
  • Any two suited cards. Being suited adds only a few percent of equity; 9♥ 3♥ is still a bad hand.
  • Small pairs out of position facing a raise: you flop a set only about 1 time in 8.5.

Frequently asked questions

How many starting hands are there in Texas Hold'em?

169 distinct hands (13 pairs, 78 suited and 78 offsuit combinations of ranks), made from 1,326 possible two-card combinations.

What is the best starting hand in Hold'em?

Pocket aces. You are dealt them 1 time in 221.

How often do you get a pocket pair?

Once every 17 hands on average, or 5.9% of the time.

What percentage of hands should I play?

At a full home table, roughly the best 15 to 25 percent, playing more from late position than early.

Is ace-king a pair?

No, but it is one of the strongest starting hands because it makes top pair with the best kicker.
